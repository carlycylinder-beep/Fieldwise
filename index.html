Data Research

The targets throughout this section are Fieldwise's own starting benchmarks for a typical engagement, not figures pulled directly from the studies below. What the research does establish is that the underlying approach, specific tracked goals, role clarity, well-designed feedback systems, and sustained training support, reliably improves performance. The citations back the method. The numbers are ours.

  1. R1 - Goal-setting and accountability. One of the most replicated findings in applied psychology: specific, difficult, tracked goals consistently outperform vague ones or "do your best" instructions, with effect sizes (d) ranging from 0.42 to 0.82 across four decades of meta-analytic replication. Locke & Latham (2002), American Psychologist.
  2. R2 - Role clarity and job performance. A meta-analysis found role ambiguity has a real, moderate negative relationship with job performance (ρ=-0.21), meaning unclear expectations measurably hurt output. Tubre & Collins (2000), Journal of Management.
  3. R3 - Feedback and accountability systems. A meta-analysis of 607 effect sizes and 23,663 observations found feedback interventions improved performance on average (d=0.41), but over a third of them made performance worse, depending on how they were designed. Poorly built accountability systems can backfire. Kluger & DeNisi (1996), Psychological Bulletin.
  4. R4 - Training effectiveness and transfer. A meta-analysis of 89 studies found that whether training actually changes on-the-job behavior depends heavily on a supportive work environment after the training ends, not just the training event itself. Blume, Ford, Baldwin & Huang (2010), Journal of Management.
  5. R5 - Lead response time and conversion. A 2007 study of over 15,000 leads and 100,000 call attempts found contacting a lead within 5 minutes versus 30 minutes made contact 100 times more likely and qualification 21 times more likely (Oldroyd, MIT Sloan / InsideSales.com). A separate 2011 audit of 2,241 US firms found the average first response took 42 hours, and contacting within an hour made qualification about 7 times more likely than waiting longer. Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington (2011), Harvard Business Review.
  6. R6 - Standardized handoffs and communication quality. Peer-reviewed patient-safety research consistently finds that standardizing how information is passed between people at a handoff point reduces errors and omissions, the same principle behind aviation and hospital safety checklists. Coon et al., Neurology: Clinical Practice; also see 150-sample team-communication meta-analysis by Marlow et al. (2018).

Do I need new software?

Probably not. Most businesses already have more software than they need. The issue is usually execution, consistency, communication, or visibility. The first step is determining how to make what you already have work better before recommending anything new.

Will you implement AI?

Only if it genuinely makes your business easier to operate. AI isn't the starting point. The focus starts with creating lasting behavioral change, accountability, and consistency in how things get done. From there, technology gets evaluated for where it can genuinely save time.

What types of businesses do you work with?

Established trade and service businesses, typically $1M-$10M+ in annual revenue, where growth is accelerating and complexity is increasing. That often looks like communication becoming more difficult, consistency getting harder to maintain across crews or locations, or leadership wanting greater visibility before small issues become expensive ones.

How quickly will I see improvements?

Many owners leave the very first conversation seeing their business differently. Some improvements happen immediately. The biggest improvements typically happen during the first 90 days, because implementation begins right away.

How much money can this save my business?

A disorganized business is quietly draining money out of your pocket every single week it stays that way. Miscommunication, rework, repetitive tasks, fragmented systems, and misaligned processes all add up, and they don't fix themselves. The goal is to stop that drain now, then keep building on it.
